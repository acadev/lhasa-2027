Workshop at SupercomputingAsia 2027 · Singapore

Scaling Long-Horizon Agentic AI Systems for Scientific Discovery

Agents that plan, act, observe, and revise their strategy across many steps break the assumptions most HPC scheduling and workflow infrastructure was designed around. LHASA brings the supercomputing community into direct contact with that problem while it is still being defined.

1–4 March 2027 Singapore Full day Papers due 24 Nov 2026 (AoE)
01

Scope and motivation

Agentic AI systems are moving from single-shot prediction toward sustained, long-horizon autonomy. In scientific discovery this shift is already visible: autonomous laboratories design, execute, and interpret experiments in closed loops; AI co-scientists traverse hypothesis generation, simulation, and validation; and multi-agent orchestration couples foundation models to solvers, databases, and physical instruments. The scientific reasoning is increasingly tractable. The systems and supercomputing substrate required to run these agents reliably over hours, days, and whole campaigns is not.

The scope of this workshop is the supercomputing and systems challenges that arise as a direct consequence of agentic, long-horizon operation, rather than specific science applications in isolation. A single agentic campaign interleaves large-model inference, classical simulation, data retrieval, and — in the autonomous-laboratory case — physical experiments, each with different latency, placement, and reservation requirements, and the composition changes dynamically as the agent decides what to do next. These workloads demand persistent state and memory that survive across steps and failures, orchestration across heterogeneous accelerators and facilities, fault tolerance measured over the lifetime of a campaign rather than a single job, provenance sufficient for a result to be trusted and reproduced, and human oversight inserted at the right points without stalling throughput.

We take the position that these are supercomputing problems first. Progress requires co-design across model developers, systems and scheduler researchers, facility operators, and the domain scientists whose loops the agents are meant to close. The workshop is deliberately cross-domain: we convene researchers working in the life sciences, materials, climate, and the physical sciences alongside the infrastructure teams at Asia-Pacific and international HPC centers, on the premise that the systems abstractions worth building are the ones that recur across all of these domains.

The Asia-Pacific setting is well matched to this agenda. The region operates several of the world's largest systems and is investing heavily in autonomous experimentation and AI-for-science platforms, and the centers co-organizing SupercomputingAsia are precisely the operators who will have to run agentic workloads in production. Our intent is that LHASA seeds a durable community and a shared research agenda — common benchmarks, failure modes, and interface conventions for agentic scientific computing — that outlasts a single meeting.

02

Topics of interest

Scheduling and resource management

Reservations, co-scheduling, and placement for campaigns that interleave inference, simulation, and instrument time with a composition that changes at runtime.

Persistent state and agent memory

State and memory that survive across steps, jobs, and failures, including checkpointing and recovery at campaign granularity.

Inference serving and orchestration

Serving large models for sustained multi-step reasoning, batching and routing under agent-driven demand, and coupling models to solvers and databases.

Heterogeneous and federated execution

Orchestration across accelerators, facilities, and edge instruments, and the movement of state between laboratories and central supercomputers.

Fault tolerance over campaigns

Failure models and recovery semantics when the unit of work is a week-long investigation rather than a single job.

Provenance and reproducibility

Recording agent decisions, tool calls, and data lineage in enough detail for a result to be trusted, audited, and reproduced.

Human oversight in the loop

Inserting review and intervention at the right points without stalling throughput, and the interfaces that make oversight practical at scale.

Energy and cost accounting

Attribution, budgeting, and accounting for long-running agentic workloads across mixed inference and simulation footprints.

Autonomous laboratories

Closing design–build–test–learn loops on HPC, including instrument integration, queueing against physical resources, and safety interlocks.

Benchmarks and evaluation

Workloads, metrics, and failure taxonomies for long-horizon agentic systems, and what a shared benchmark suite would need to capture.

Program structure

01

Invited keynotes

Two to three talks framing the systems challenges from the model, facility, and domain-science perspectives.

02

Reviewed paper track

Full and short papers presented orally.

03

Lightning and position papers

A fast session to surface early ideas and open problems.

04

Closing panel

“What does an operating system for agentic science look like?” — a foundation-model developer, a scheduler and systems researcher, an HPC facility operator, and a domain scientist, moderated by the organizers.

Invited perspectives under discussion cover inference orchestration and serving for long-horizon agents, closing design–build–test–learn loops in autonomous laboratories on HPC, scheduling heterogeneous agentic workloads from a facility-operations standpoint, and long-horizon agents in the life and materials sciences. After the workshop we will circulate a short community report drawn from the panel and the position papers, so that the meeting leaves a persistent set of open problems behind it.
